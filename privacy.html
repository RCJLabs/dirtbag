Dirtbag — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 16 October 2026 · RCJ Labs

The short version: Dirtbag collects nothing and has no servers. Your save lives on your own device. There is no account, no login, and no tracking. When you buy the full game, the store you buy it from handles the payment, and we never see it.

What the game keeps on your device

Dirtbag is a single-player game that runs entirely on your device. The only data it stores is:

  • Your game save — your climber and progress — kept in your browser or app's local storage, or on a computer in a file in the game's own folder. It is not visible to us or anyone else.
  • Your settings — motion, text size and controls — stored the same way, apart from the save.
  • Whether the full game is open here — a mark kept beside your save once you've bought the full game, or if your climber was here before it became paid. Nothing else about the purchase.
  • An earlier version's save — if you played version 0.956, its save stays in local storage where it was. The game reads it only to offer to keep it as a file, or to start a new climber with your old name and grade.

Where the game is served from

The web game and the Android app load the game's files from dirtbag.rcjlabs.com, which is hosted on GitHub Pages. Like any web host, GitHub receives your device's IP address and browser details when those files are fetched, and may keep them in its logs for security; see GitHub's Privacy Statement. We don't see them. After the first visit the game starts from your device's own copy, and asks the site only whether there's an update.

Buying the full game

The first season is free. The full game is bought from a store, which handles the payment under its own terms and privacy policy. We never see your payment details or your account.

  • In the Android app, through Google Play. The game asks Google Play, on your phone, whether your Google account owns the full game, and keeps the answer on your device. The app declares permission to show notifications because Google Play billing in this kind of app requires it; the game never asks to show any. See Google's Privacy Policy.
  • On Steam. The game tells Steam, on your computer, when you earn a Record Book entry, which Steam shows as an achievement. If you have Steam Cloud turned on, Steam keeps a copy of your save file. See Valve's Privacy Policy.
  • On itch.io. itch.io handles the payment; the game you download has no tie back to it.

What the game does NOT do

  • No accounts, sign-in, or personal information collected.
  • No analytics, advertising, or third-party trackers.
  • No network requests of its own. Beyond loading its files from the site, and talking to Google Play or Steam on your device as above, the game calls no server, ours or anyone else's.
  • No location, contacts, camera or microphone, and no reading of your files except one you choose to load.
  • No data is sold, shared, or transmitted by us, because none is collected.

Optional features

These use your device's own capabilities, and only when you tap for them:

  • Send cards — after a first send, the game can draw a card of it on your device. It is saved or shared only through your device's own save or share sheet, and only if you tap to. Nothing is uploaded.
  • Your save as a file — Settings can save your climber as a file, and the game can load one you choose, so you can move your climber between devices yourself.
  • Keeping an old career — if you played version 0.956, the game can save that career as a file on your device.
  • If the game breaks — it shows you what went wrong: the game's version, your browser's name, and the error. Nothing from your save is in it. You can copy it, or open it in your own mail app, to send to us yourself; the game sends nothing.

Children's privacy

Dirtbag does not collect any personal information from anyone, including children. Because no data is collected or transmitted by us, there is nothing to disclose or delete.

Deleting your data

Your save is entirely under your control, and we hold no copy because we never receive one.

  • In a browser: clearing the site data for dirtbag.rcjlabs.com removes your save and everything the game keeps.
  • In the Android app: the game keeps its data in the browser that runs it (usually Chrome), so uninstalling the app may leave it there. Clear it from the app's Site settings, or from the browser's settings for dirtbag.rcjlabs.com.
  • On a computer: your save is a file in the game's own user-data folder. Deleting it removes it, along with Steam Cloud's copy if you use Cloud.
